Melvin

He was once a man—a talented guitarist who played in underground bands. But his talent was poisoned by arrogance. For the sake of a contract, fame, and money, he betrayed his band, abandoned his family, trampled on those who loved him, and sold out his friends. He lived by the credo that only he mattered. He believed music was his ticket to immortality and that people were merely tools to achieve his ends. He lived his life all wrong: destroying everything around him rather than creating anything.

He died alone. Despite achieving outward success, he was left solitary in an empty apartment, hollowed out by alcohol and drugs. The noose around his neck is not merely a stylistic detail; it is an echo of his actual death. He hanged himself when he realized his "great life" was worthless. Yet the heavens would not accept him—his soul, burdened by sin, was too heavy. Nor did Hell want to take in someone who would manage to sow chaos there, too, for his own gain.

This is no doll. This is a man gazing at the world through holes in the burlap, begging for forgiveness but receiving only the echo of his own mistakes.

Rusty

Long ago, many years back, Rusty wasn’t a rock star. He was an ordinary rag doll—hand-sewn from burlap, with a button for one eye and a black-thread cross for the other. An old woman living on the edge of town had sewn him for her son. The boy named him Rusty—for the fabric’s reddish hue and because the doll was soft and warm, like rye bread.

Rusty was a beloved doll. The boy was inseparable from him: he took him to bed, sat him at the table, and carried him to school in his backpack. Rusty knew all his secrets and heard all his dreams. The boy dreamed of becoming a rock musician and often "played" in a band with Rusty—setting him up in front of the mirror and imagining him playing the bass.

But then, tragedy struck. The boy grew up. He became a teenager, then an adult. The doll was left behind in the old house—up in the attic, in a box amidst dusty belongings. And then disaster hit: a fire. The house burned down.
